# LibSwiftRide three-year financial model inputs

This brief defines the inputs required before LibSwiftRide publishes financial projections, a funding requirement, an equity proposal, a break-even estimate, or an investor-return scenario. It intentionally contains no invented financial results.

## Operating assumptions required

- Launch month and service-area sequence
- Active riders by month, ride frequency, retention and seasonality
- Approved fare assumptions by ride type
- Completed-trip volume, cancellation rate and refund rate
- Driver onboarding, activation and retention assumptions
- Confirmed 86% driver earnings and 14% platform commission; qualified first-ride referrals use 2% of that fare as a platform-funded reward
- Business-account acquisition, contract structure and payment terms
- Delivery, logistics or other future-service launch timing

## Cost assumptions required

- Product development and cloud infrastructure
- Mapping, GPS, communications and notification providers
- Payment-provider fees and settlement costs
- Driver verification, onboarding and vehicle-readiness costs
- Operations, dispatch, customer support, finance and compliance staffing
- Marketing and customer-acquisition budgets
- Office, equipment, insurance, legal and administrative costs
- Safety operations, incident response and monitoring
- Taxes, statutory obligations and contingency reserves

## Financing decisions required

- Target funding amount and minimum viable close
- Funding instrument: equity, convertible instrument, debt or blended structure
- Pre-money valuation or valuation methodology
- Proposed investor ownership and dilution limits
- Founder and employee ownership assumptions
- Use-of-funds allocation and release milestones
- Follow-on financing assumptions

## Model outputs

The approved model should produce monthly detail for 36 months and annual summaries for:

- Gross booking value
- Driver earnings
- Platform commission revenue
- Business mobility revenue
- Other service revenue
- Direct service costs
- Gross margin
- Operating expenses
- EBITDA and operating cash flow
- Cash balance and runway
- Break-even month
- Funding requirement
- Rider, driver, business-account and trip growth
- Base, downside and upside scenarios

## Investor-return analysis

Investor returns must be scenario-based and should show assumptions for valuation, dilution, follow-on rounds, holding period and potential exit value. The model must not describe any return as guaranteed.
